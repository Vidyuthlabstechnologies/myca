# Screen-by-screen UX pass, 18 October 2026

| | |
|---|---|
| Scope | Every screen in `mobile/lib/screens` (45 files) read as a first-time owner; the desktop renderer walked in a browser on a fresh data folder |
| Method | Every user-visible string listed per screen; the taps from Home to a posted sale counted; defaults, dead ends, icon labels and error surfacing checked in code |
| Outcome | Copy is already in the owner's words throughout; four changes made (below); the rest is recorded here as verdicts so the next pass does not repeat the reading |

## Changes made

| Screen | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Customer and supplier thread (`party_screen.dart`) | The receipt form now prefills the amount with what is owed on the oldest unpaid invoice, as it already did for bills | A receipt is almost always the full invoice; typing it again was the slowest tap in the daily flow |
| Customer and supplier thread | A sale or quote prefills the GST rate with the one last used for that party; a new party gets no rate, and the engine still refuses to guess | The rate chips were one tap away; most sales to the same customer are at the same rate |
| Ask | Starter questions now include the eight new workflows and one Hinglish line ("Dhanda kaisa chal raha hai?") | Owners discover what the app can do by tapping; the Hinglish line says plainly that it understands them |
| Desktop (`platform/desktop/renderer`) | First-run setup with state and PAN from the GSTIN, then "Where are your books today?" | The desktop opened on the sample company with no way to find the setup |

## Verdicts by journey

**First open to first invoice.** Welcome (three lines, one button, "about two minutes") → Google or phone sign-in → name → business (GSTIN derives the state; an unregistered business picks one) → where are your books (five choices, skip allowed) → Home with "Log your first sale". Under three minutes with a GSTIN. No jargon found. One wording to keep: "Nothing reaches your books until you say so" is the promise the whole app keeps.

**A day's work.** Home → What happened? → I sold / bought / paid → pick or add the party → amount, rate, what → Preview → Post: six taps plus the amount. The expense form asks for the amount "with GST" (how a receipt reads) while the sale form asks "before GST" (how an invoice is built); both say which. The head is automatic and remembered per payee. The bill photo path says plainly when the model is off and what to do instead.

**Month end.** Taxes shows each return with its due date and how late it is; the review asks "Is this information correct?" step by step and reopens itself when the books change; GSTR-2B is added with a preview; the supplier reminder is drafted from their own bill numbers and handed to the phone; the GST payment records the challan; a filing records the ARN and stops the nagging. Nothing files from the phone, and every screen says so.

**Payroll, cash, people, CA.** Payroll is a draft until approved, with "Check:" lines; Cash leads with "Tell MyCA your balance today" when there is no reading; Who to pay and Remind customer are one tap from Cash and from the customer's thread; People explains each role in one sentence and what the accountant sees; CA questions carry their own attachments and can be copied for a CA not on MyCA.

**Dead ends.** Every failure found has a next action: "Try again", "add it by hand from Purchases", "give your balance on the Cash screen", "approve a salary month first". No raw codes reach the screen; the engine's codes are worded in the router.

**Accessibility.** Every icon button carries a tooltip; buttons are 50 points high; meaning is never carried by colour alone (warn and danger colours always sit on words); the Inter face at 14 to 16 points. Not checked on a device: TalkBack order and dynamic type at the largest setting, which the first device run should cover.

## Device checks (18 October 2026, evening)

Two kinds, both now repeatable.

**Automated, in the suite.** `mobile/test/accessibility_test.dart` walks fourteen screens
(welcome to Home, the business menu, what happened, who did you sell to, the customer
thread with the sale form, the sales register, Taxes, the return review, Expenses and the
expense form, Cash, Who to pay, Ask) and on each asserts Flutter's Android tap-target
guideline (48 points), the labelled-tap-target guideline and the text-contrast guideline,
then renders the same screen at text scale 2.0 and asserts no layout error. Two real
findings, both fixed: the business tile in the Home app bar was a 34-point tap target
(now 48), and the rupee prefix in amount fields was drawn in the hint colour at a
contrast of 2.7 (now ink, like the value). All 28 checks pass; the suite is 211 tests.

**On an emulator (Android 15, 420 dpi, TalkBack installed).** The debug build was installed
and driven through welcome, sign-in, the business form, "Where are your books today?",
"You are all set", Home and the new-entry sheet, reading the accessibility tree the way a
screen reader would and screenshotting at font scale 1.0 and 2.0. What it showed: every
row is announced as its title and its subtitle together ("I sold something, Raise an
invoice for a customer"); every control is announced by name ("Your business",
"New entry", "Send", "Continue", "Open my books"); tap targets are 48 points except the
one the suite also caught; at font scale 2.0 Home, the business form, the migration list
and the new-entry sheet grow without clipping or overflow, the greeting truncates with an
ellipsis, and the floating button covers the bottom card until the page is scrolled, as
on every Material app. Two things to verify on a physical phone, because the emulator
cannot: TalkBack's spoken hint for empty text fields (the tree shows the label as a hint,
which TalkBack reads and the dump tool does not), and the ANR the debug build hit while
the host was overloaded, which did not recur once the load dropped.
